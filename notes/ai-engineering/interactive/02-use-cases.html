2.3Where AI helps developers (and where it doesn't)

Coding is "hands down the most popular use case" — but the productivity gain depends on the task. The pattern generalizes: AI shines on simple, well-bounded work; gains fade as complexity rises.

Related signals from the chapter: developers say AI is better at frontend than backend; companies can do more with fewer engineers; the outsourcing industry is exposed (its tasks skew simple); and the replacement debate spans Jensen Huang's "stop saying kids should learn to code" to AWS's Matt Garman: most developers will stop coding — the job changes, it doesn't vanish.

2.5How enterprises actually deploy

Internal-facing first

  • a16z Growth (2024): companies ship internal apps (knowledge management) faster than external ones (support chatbots).
  • Internal apps build AI expertise while limiting risks: data privacy, compliance, catastrophic failures.

Close-ended over open-ended

  • Foundation models are open-ended, but many apps built on them stay close-ended (e.g., classification).
  • Close-ended tasks are easier to evaluate → risks easier to estimate.
In the early internet, few predicted social media would dominate. "As we learn to make the most out of AI, the use case that will eventually dominate might surprise us."
