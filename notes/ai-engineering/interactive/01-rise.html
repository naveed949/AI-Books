1.2Tokens — the basic unit

A language model's basic unit is the token: a character, a word, or part of a word (like -tion). Breaking text into tokens is tokenization; the set of all tokens a model can use is its vocabulary (Mixtral 8×7B: 32,000 · GPT-4: 100,256).

1.3Two kinds of language model

They differ by what context they may use to predict a token. Toggle between them:

My favorite color is ___

Predicts the next token using only preceding tokens — then generates one token after another. The model of choice for text generation, and far more popular today. Sometimes called a causal language model. In the book, "language model" means this by default.

My favorite ___ is blue

Fills in the blank using context from both sides. Classic example: BERT (Devlin et al., 2018). Common for non-generative tasks — sentiment analysis, text classification — and tasks needing whole-context understanding, like code debugging.

A language model is a completion machine: given a prompt, it predicts what comes next — probabilistically, with no guarantee of correctness. Translation, summarization, coding, math, and spam classification can all be framed as completion. But completion ≠ conversation: ask a raw completion machine a question and it may "complete" you with another question. Making models respond appropriately is what post-training is for.

1.4Self-supervision — the unlock that enabled scale

Why did language models (not recommenders, not forecasters) become the center of the scaling race? Because they can be trained with self-supervision: each text sequence supplies both the contexts and the labels. No human labeling — so training data is effectively unlimited.

1.5"Large" is a moving target

A model's size is measured by its parameters — variables updated during training. What counts as "large" keeps being revised upward. (Larger models have more capacity to learn, so they need more data to reach their potential — training a big model on a small dataset just wastes compute.)

1.6From LLMs to foundation models

Why "foundation"?

  • Text-only models can't perceive the world the way humans do — so models absorbed more modalities (GPT-4V and Claude 3: images; others: video, 3D, protein structures).
  • "Foundation" = both their importance and that they can be built upon.
  • Breaks the old modality split of AI research (NLP / computer vision / audio).
  • Multimodal model = handles >1 modality; generative multimodal = LMM. The book's "foundation models" covers both LLMs and LMMs.

CLIP — self-supervision goes multimodal

  • OpenAI's CLIP (2021) used natural language supervision: 400M (image, text) pairs found co-occurring on the internet — 400× ImageNet, zero labeling cost.
  • First model to generalize to many image-classification tasks without extra training.
  • CLIP is not generative — it's an embedding model (vectors that capture meaning), the backbone behind Flamingo, LLaVA, Gemini.
Foundation models also flipped AI from task-specific to general-purpose. One model does sentiment analysis and translation. To push it further on your task, three adaptation techniques recur all book long: prompt engineering, RAG, and finetuning — "ten examples and one weekend versus 1 million examples and six months."

1.7Three factors behind the boom

AI engineering = building apps on foundation models. Building AI apps isn't new (ML engineering / MLOps) — but three conditions made this moment different. Expand each:

Factor 1 — General-purpose capabilities
Foundation models don't just do old tasks better — they do more tasks. AI now writes at or above human level, so it can (partially) automate anything requiring communication — "pretty much everything": emails, support, contracts, marketing visuals, code, even synthesizing training data for future models. More useful → more users → more demand.
Factor 2 — Increased AI investments
ChatGPT triggered a rush: Goldman Sachs projected AI investment approaching $100B in the US / $200B globally by 2025. 1 in 3 S&P 500 companies mentioned AI on Q2 2023 earnings calls (3× the prior year); those that did saw stocks rise 4.6% vs 2.4% (causation vs. correlation unclear). Scribd's estimated AI cost fell two orders of magnitude in one year (Apr 2022 → Apr 2023).
Factor 3 — Low entrance barrier
Model as a service: powerful models behind a single API call — no serving infrastructure needed. Plus AI writes code and speaks plain English, so "anyone, and I mean anyone, can now develop AI applications." Building the models themselves stays with big corporations, governments, and well-funded startups — Sam Altman: the biggest opportunity for most people is adapting these models. Proof of the gold rush: within 2 years, AutoGPT, Stable Diffusion Web UI, LangChain, and Ollama each out-starred Bitcoin on GitHub.

Why call it "AI engineering"?

Not ML engineering — foundation-model work differs too much from traditional ML (though it's a fine umbrella term). Not the -Ops names (MLOps, LLMOps) — the focus is on engineering models to do what you want, not just operations. And when Huyen surveyed 20 practitioners, most preferred AI engineering. "I decided to go with the people."
