Chapter 1 — Introduction to Building AI Applications with Foundation Models

An interactive study companion. Four short modules plus a quiz — each built around the chapter's own examples, numbers, and frameworks so the ideas stick fast.

The one-word thesis: scale. Scaling made models more capable (more demand for AI apps) while model as a service made them accessible (lower barrier to entry). That combination — demand up, barrier down — is what turned AI engineering (building applications on top of readily available models) into one of the fastest-growing engineering disciplines. How the chapter fits together

One sentence per section:

  • The Rise of AI Engineering — self-supervision let language models scale into LLMs, more modalities made them foundation models, and model-as-a-service turned building on them into a discipline.
  • Foundation Model Use Cases — eight recurring patterns (coding, media, writing, education, bots, information aggregation, data organization, workflow automation) across consumer and enterprise, with enterprises preferring lower-risk, internal-facing, close-ended apps.
  • Planning AI Applications — decide why (risk vs. opportunity), define the role of AI and humans, plan for defensibility, metrics, the last mile, and maintenance.
  • The AI Engineering Stack — application development, model development, and infrastructure; AI engineering is less about training models and more about adapting and evaluating them.
